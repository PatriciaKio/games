Phase 1 Research
Academic Prompt & Research Question
How do games support learning outcomes? What does peer-reviewed research say about game-based learning in education — specifically for sustainability and environmental topics?
Peer-Reviewed Literature Review
GBL Foundations
Plass, Homer & Kinzer (2015) — challenge, response, feedback as three pillars of GBL. Cognitive, affective, motivational & sociocultural engagement.
GBL Outcomes
Frontiers in Psychology meta-analysis (2024) — moderate-to-large effect on motivation, engagement & cognitive performance; especially effective for lower prior-knowledge learners.
Games + Sustainability
Tan et al. (2023); ILE two-phase review (2024); Frontiers in Education (2025) — serious games increase environmental knowledge, pro-environmental attitudes & behaviour change.
Key Research Findings Applied to Design
Immediate feedback loops drive retention — games must show consequences of choices visibly and quickly (Plass et al., 2015)
Delayed feedback mirrors real ecological systems and builds systems thinking (Tan et al., 2023)
Player agency and role identity deepen engagement and learning transfer — being "someone" in the game matters (Plass et al., 2015)
Failure as learning — corrective debrief after failure significantly improves knowledge retention (Mayer & Johnson, 2010)

Phase 2 Game Concept
Discussion Prompts (Assignment Requirements)
Q1 · Game Concept
What is the theme, story, or purpose? What experience should the player have? What challenge or problem drives the gameplay?
Q2 · Core Mechanic
What main action will players repeat throughout the game? Define how the game functions moment-to-moment (resource management, puzzle-solving, timing, etc.)
EcoBalance — Answers to Discussion Prompts
Concept & Theme
Player is City Sustainability Director of Verdana, a growing fictional city. Theme: the real tension between economic growth and ecological responsibility. Purpose: felt understanding of sustainability systems — not just facts.

Challenge driving gameplay: Manage 4 interdependent meters (Carbon, Economy, Community Wellbeing, Biodiversity) over a 10-year in-game timeline without letting any fall into the red.
Core Mechanic
Decision-driven resource management with delayed consequence feedback.

Each round: draw 3 policy cards, evaluate trade-offs across 4 meters, choose 1, allocate Green Credits budget. Some effects are immediate; others are queued 1–3 months later — teaching that sustainability outcomes are delayed (research-backed).

Moment-to-moment: Draw → Evaluate → Decide → Observe consequences → Adapt
Research connection: player role identity (Plass 2015) + delayed feedback mirrors ecological cycles (Tan 2023)
Phase 3 Game Spec Sheet
One-Page Game Spec — EcoBalance · Sustainability Simulation
1
Learning Target
By end of playthrough, player will:
• Explain the 3 pillars of sustainability (environmental, economic, social)
• Describe 3+ cause-and-effect relationships between decisions and outcomes
• Articulate why sustainability requires systems thinking, not single fixes
2
Player Role
City Sustainability Director of Verdana.

Player has real policy agency — every card played shapes the city's trajectory. Role creates ownership and identity investment, which research links to deeper engagement and learning transfer.
3
Core Loop
① Draw 3 Policy Cards
↓
② Evaluate trade-offs across 4 meters
↓
③ Choose 1 card + allocate Green Credits
↓
④ Watch immediate + delayed effects
↓
⑤ Read Verdana Gazette report → Adapt
4
Feedback & Score Metrics
4 Live Meters:
Carbon Index
Economy Score
Community Wellbeing %
Biodiversity Rating
Composite Sustainability Score: weighted A–F letter grade updated each round.

3-layer feedback:
• Immediate — meter animations after each card
• Monthly — Verdana Gazette headline (plain-language ripple effects)
• Yearly — report card with trend chart + 1 recommended adjustment
5
Win Condition & Learn Condition
WIN: Reach Year 10 with all 4 meters ≥ 60% AND Sustainability Score of B or higher.

CRISIS MODE: If any meter hits red for 3+ consecutive months, city enters crisis — compounding consequences simulate the real cost of delayed action. Player can still recover.

LEARN CONDITION (on failure): "What Happened?" debrief screen shows exactly which decisions created the cascade — turning failure into an explicit, citable learning moment.

Research basis: corrective post-failure feedback improves retention (Mayer & Johnson, 2010)
Phase 4 AI Refine
AI LLM Prompt & Iterative Negotiation
Step 1 · Prompt
Use spec sheet as input prompt. Instruct LLM to produce a 1–1.5 page game spec doc matching all 5 elements. Be explicit about format and tone.
Step 2 · Review
Read output critically. Does it preserve the research-backed design choices? Is the learning target measurable? Are all 5 spec elements present and clear?
Step 3 · Negotiate
Push back on weak sections. Refine through dialogue until output matches vision. Accept only when every element reflects both the assignment requirements and the research.
